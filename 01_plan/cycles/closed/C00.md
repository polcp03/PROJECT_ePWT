and splitting it with a 2.5-week gap is not a cycle: it is a broken cycle.

So C00 does **one thing only**: set up the relationship with the mentors before you leave, so
that on 28 September you start working instead of organising.

> Going with a plan and nothing else reproduces exactly the pattern you want to fix.
> With **one real measurement**, the meeting changes from "this is what I am going to do" to
> "this is what I have already started". It is another conversation.

> [!warning] Two corrections to the original plan
> **The cogging method for pole pairs was wrong.** The cogging positions are set by the
> interaction between stator slots and rotor magnets — typically `lcm(slots, poles)` — not by
> `p`. It would have given a plausible and false number. Counting Hall pulses does measure `p`
> by definition: one Hall line gives exactly `p` cycles per mechanical turn.
>
> **The `÷2` of the phase-phase resistance was right**, confirmed by reading the firmware:
> `foc_motor_r` is resistance per phase. But the expected value (57.6 mΩ between phases) is
> below what a normal multimeter resolves, whose leads alone are 0.1–0.5 Ω. It needs a known
> current source or a milliohmmeter. Parked, not discarded.

## The slide that matters most: what I expect from them

They themselves said they would learn from you. That is fine, but it can dilute the meeting
into a pleasant technical chat with no edge. You have to ask explicitly for what you need:

> [!quote] What I ask of them
> 1. **Hold me to a date.** Without an external deadline, I postpone.
> 2. **Attack my reasoning**, not the result. Does the logic hold?
> 3. **Spot when I explain badly** because I haven't understood.
>
> What I do NOT ask: validating FOC or electric motor control. I cover that gap another way,
> and saying so openly keeps them from feeling assessed in a domain that is not theirs.

## Close

**Result: the meeting went well and the pre-cycle met its only goal.** The mentors got hooked
and want to see how it evolves. That is exactly what C00 had to buy: an open relationship before
the holidays, so that on 28 September work starts instead of organising.

**Mentors' feedback:** what they liked most **was not the measurements, it was the method** —
how the project is organised, the two-week cycle, the traceability. It is worth reading that
carefully: they are combustion engineers, they cannot validate FOC, and the deck told them so.
What they can assess is whether an engineer works in a defensible way. That they bit right
there means slide 13's framing worked.

**Consequence for C01:** if the system is what they value, the 12 October review has to show
the system **producing a result**, not the system again. The second time, the method no longer
impresses: the numbers do.
