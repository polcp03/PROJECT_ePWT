VCU perfboard

Today's ESP32, CAN module, microSD and wheel, plus the control unit's throttle, brakes and IMU, at real scale: each dot is a 2.54 mm hole. Tap a wire or a part to see what it is and why it is there. No module gets desoldered: the SD stands upright on its side pins and the CAN sits on standoffs, with a cable.

View from above (component side). The wires run under the board.

Wires

Grey track with a coloured line = bare copper wire (buses only). Coloured wire = insulated wire: it can cross over another one. Dashed = control unit, soldered later.

The modules, nothing desoldered

  • microSD: standing. Its pins come out sideways (right angle). They plug as they are into a straight female header soldered on the board, and the module stands vertical, like a card in a slot. It takes a single row, rises 42 mm and the card goes in from the top. If the pins end up reversed from the drawing, turn the module 180°: the order flips.
  • CAN: lying on standoffs, with a cable. Its pins face up, so it cannot be plugged in. It sits on 4 nylon 10 mm standoffs (if your module has no holes, thick double-sided tape). From its 7 pins a ribbon of 7 female-female 10 cm dupont wires, all together, goes to a male header on the board. A drop of hot glue at each end so they don't come loose.
  • ESP32: plugged into two 19-pin female headers, with the rows 10 holes apart.

The IMU goes on the board (DEC-008)

Changed on 6-nov. Until then the IMU sat on the chassis at the end of a cable. An I2C cable is an antenna next to the VESC: open-drain and single-ended, so noise corrupts bits or locks the bus. On the board there is no cable.

  • Where: a 1×8 female socket at column 36, rows 6–13, right of the ESP32. Four pins are wired: VCC, GND, SCL, SDA.
  • Pin order: drawn as VCC, GND, SCL, SDA, SDO, CS, INT1, INT2. Check your module's silkscreen before soldering and move the wires if it differs.
  • SDO and CS: for I2C at 0x6A, SDO goes to GND and CS to 3V3. Most GY-LSM6DS3 modules do this on board: check with the multimeter. If yours doesn't, bridge them on the module.
  • Pull-ups: the module usually carries its own (often 10 kΩ). With no cable, at 100 kHz, they are enough. Only if it has none, add 4.7 kΩ to 3V3 (R8, R9 on sheet 2).
  • The box is now the IMU's mount: bolt the VCU box rigidly to the chassis, near the middle of the quad, with no rubber or foam. Mark X forward and Z up on the box, so the code does not have to rotate anything.
  • Soldering its pins without breaking it: pins pushed into the breadboard, module on top, fine tip at ~330 °C and 2 seconds per pin. Never lever with the iron.

Build order

  1. Mark the board. 9×15 cm double-sided: the layout takes 44×34 holes (112×86 mm). Cut off the rest or leave it as margin.
  2. Sockets: 2×19 female for the ESP32, 1×6 female for the SD and 1×7 male for the CAN cable. Solder with the module plugged in, so they stay straight.
  3. Terminal blocks on the edges, screws facing out.
  4. Bare buses: GND (row 2), +5 V (row 4) and +3.3 V (row 6). Ground continues along the left edge and the bottom.
  5. Test with nothing plugged in: continuity between +5 V and GND, and between +3.3 V and GND. It must not beep.
  6. Power to each socket and capacitors C6, C7 and C8. DC-DC to J1: measure 5.3 V on the ESP32's 5V pin, on the CAN's VCC and on the SD's, still without modules.
  7. CAN SPI with R1 and R2, and SD SPI. Short wires, flat against the board.
  8. Wheel: J3 and R3.
  9. Plug in the modules and record with the wheel turned by hand. Then with throttle: 0 corrupt frames, as in EXP-002.
  10. Later: J2 and J4 with their resistors, the IMU socket and its 4 wires, and C9 (1 nF) on the wheel input. The holes are already reserved.

Electrical review

  • okEvery pin, against Espressif's documentation. Left column from 3V3 to 5V and right from GND to CLK, as on the ESP32-DevKitC V4. They match vcu-pinout.md: wheel 34, throttle 35, brakes 32/33, CAN 14/13/27/26, SD 18/19/23/5, IMU 21/22.
  • okNo ESP32 input sees 5 V. The CAN's SO comes down through R1/R2 to 3.3 V; the throttle is powered at 3.3 V (2.60 V maximum measured); the pull-ups go to +3.3 V. The SD module has its own regulator and its outputs are already 3.3 V.
  • okDelicate pins left free. 12 without a pull-up (otherwise it won't boot), 6 to 11 untouched (flash memory), 1 and 3 for USB. 5 is a strapping pin, but CS rests high.
  • okAnalog inputs on ADC1 (35 and the free 36/39): ADC2 doesn't read with Wi-Fi on.
  • okA single ground, which touches the vehicle only through J5 (DEC-004). The board floats on its 12 V battery.
  • okFiltering: 220 µF at the input and 100 nF next to each module and each sensor.
  • riskThe CAN module gets 3.3 V on its SPI inputs while powered at 5 V. If the MCP2515's input threshold is 85 % of its supply (that is how I remember it, but I couldn't check it in the datasheet), 3.3 V is below what the manufacturer guarantees. It works: EXP-002 recorded 33,783 frames without an error. If odd errors ever show up, it is the first thing to look at.
  • to measureConsumption: ESP32 with Wi-Fi (~250 mA peaks), CAN (~70 mA) and SD writing (~100 mA peaks) add up to ~0.5 A. Check that your DC-DC gives at least 1 A.
  • to measureInterference (DEC-008): the box under the chassis plate, cables leaving on the side away from the VESC; CAN twisted with its GND; the wheel Hall on the shielded cable, shield to GND at the board only; IMU on the board, no cable. Measured in EXP-004.

What if I have the board made?

It is doable and cheap. It is designed in KiCad (free) and ordered from a maker like JLCPCB: 5 two-layer boards up to 100×100 mm cost about $2, plus $10–15 shipping to Spain and sometimes import VAT. They arrive in 1–2 weeks.

  • For: no wires coming loose and no crossings, smaller (fits in 100×100 mm) and repeatable. And designing a PCB in KiCad is a skill systems engineering jobs in robotics ask for: it goes in the portfolio.
  • Against: 2–4 design sessions and 1–2 weeks of waiting, and if there is a mistake, another round. And the control unit circuit (throttle, brakes, IMU) hasn't been tested yet.
  • My recommendation: perfboard now, so the logger records straight away and the full circuit gets tested. The PCB, once the control unit works on the perfboard: you move to KiCad something you already know works.
  • A question for then: the ESP32 already has a CAN controller inside (it is called TWAI; it is what VESC Express uses). With a 3.3 V transceiver like the SN65HVD230 (~€2) the MCP2515 would not be needed: no SPI, no divider and none of the level risk above. It would change the firmware's can_rx module. We decide it together before designing the PCB.
